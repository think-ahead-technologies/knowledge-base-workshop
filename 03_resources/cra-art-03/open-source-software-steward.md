# CRA free and open-source software and its steward

"Freie und quelloffene Software" (point 48) is software whose source code is openly shared and made available under a free open-source licence granting all rights to make it freely accessible, usable, modifiable and redistributable.

"Verwalter quelloffener Software" (open-source software steward, point 14) is a legal person, not a manufacturer, whose purpose or objective is to systematically and sustainably support the development of specific products with digital elements that qualify as FOSS and are intended for commercial activities, and that ensures the viability of those products.

Commission guidance (non-binding) sections 3.1-3.2: the decisive factor for FOSS being "placed on the market" is monetisation by the party in control (maintainer), not funding of development. Price for binaries, monetising other services via the software, mandatory data processing, or paid versions with support advantages mean placing on the market. Voluntary donations, optional consulting and third-party sponsoring do not by themselves. Point 48 requires cumulatively a FOSS licence with all rights and publicly shared source - code only for paying customers is not FOSS. Responsible is whoever controls releases, roadmap and distribution; contributors without that control are not subject to the CRA even with commit rights. Community and paid open-core versions are two different products. Donations become critical only when they are a de facto access condition (e.g. releases or security fixes only for donors); cost recovery including reasonable living costs is allowed. Third-party funding (grants, bug bounties, paid feature development) does not make FOSS commercial (recital 18).
