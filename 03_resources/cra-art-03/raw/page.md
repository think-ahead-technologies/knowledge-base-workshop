**Article 3 – Definitions (Kapitel I, Verordnung (EU) 2024/2847)**

The 51 definitions fall into these groups:

- **Core product terms (1–11):** "Produkt mit digitalen Elementen" (1), "Datenfernverarbeitung" (2), "Software" (4), "Hardware" (5), "Komponente" (6), and terms for connections and endpoints (8–11).
- **Economic operators (12–20):** manufacturer (13), authorised representative (15), importer (16), distributor (17), consumer (18), micro/small/medium enterprises (19), and the support period (20).
- **Placing on the market (21–23):** "Inverkehrbringen" (21), "Bereitstellung auf dem Markt" (22), and intended purpose (23).
- **Conformity assessment and standards (26–36):** notifying authority, conformity assessment body, notified body, "wesentliche Änderung" (30), CE marking (31), and the references to other EU legislation and standards.
- **Security and vulnerabilities (37–46):** cybersecurity risk (37), significant cybersecurity risk (38), software bill of materials (39), vulnerability (40), exploitable and actively exploited vulnerability (41–42), security incident (43–44), near-miss (45), and cyber threat (46).
- **Other (47–51):** personal data (47), free and open-source software (48), recall and withdrawal (49–50), and the coordinator CSIRT (51).

**Commission guidance (27 July 2026, C(2026) 5252 final, non-binding)**

- **2.1 Standalone software placed on the market:** the date of first offer applies to all copies of a version. Variants are separate products.
- **2.2 Software as a product:** locally installed applications are products. Browser-only web apps are not, but their back-end services may fall under Remote Data Processing.
- **2.3 Computer code:** source code supplied commercially counts as placed on the market. Public FOSS, demo code and beta versions generally do not.
- **2.4 Hardware and software as one product:** companion apps and drivers that are necessary for the product's functions form a single product with the hardware.
- **2.5 Data connection:** the CRA applies only where coded data is deliberately sent or received, not merely where electronics switch or carry power.
- **3.1–3.2 Open source:** monetisation by the maintainer, not funding alone, determines whether FOSS is placed on the market.
- **4.3 Software updates as substantial modification:** the test is the effect on the risk profile. Security updates are generally not substantial.
- **8.1 Remote Data Processing Solutions (RDPS):** the test is whether the service is needed for a product function and was developed by or for the manufacturer.
- **8.3 Five RDPS use cases:** banking app, smart thermostat, e-reader, industrial robot and mobile network, each illustrating the boundary.

**Related recitals**

Recitals 7–10, 13–16 concern the aim of raising overall cybersecurity in the internal market, risks from connected products, Member States' role in national security, and the scope limited to products made available on the market in a commercial context.
