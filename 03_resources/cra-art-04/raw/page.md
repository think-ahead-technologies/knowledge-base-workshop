**Article 4 - Freier Verkehr (Chapter I, General Provisions)**
Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act), published 10 December 2024. The page notes it was last reviewed by Kunnus in March 2026.

**Legal text (summarized)**
1. Member States may not obstruct the making available on the market of products with digital elements that comply with the Regulation, in the aspects it covers.
2. Member States may not prevent non-compliant products from being shown or demonstrated at trade fairs, exhibitions, demonstrations, or similar events, including prototypes. They must carry a visible label stating they do not comply and may only be placed on the market once they do.
3. Member States may not prevent unfinished non-compliant software from being made available for a limited period needed for testing. It must carry a visible notice that it does not comply and will not be marketed except for testing.
4. Paragraph 3 does not apply to safety components covered by other Union harmonisation legislation.

**Commission guidance (27 July 2026, C(2026) 5252 final), Section 2.3: Computer code**
- Both machine code and source code count as "software" under Article 3(4).
- What matters is whether the code is supplied in the course of a business activity.
- Publicly shared FOSS, unfinished development code, and demo or tutorial code are not considered placed on the market. Source code licensed commercially to customers is.
- A licensor is not responsible for CRA compliance of customer modifications made downstream.
- Unfinished software (alpha, beta, release candidate) may be provided for a limited time for testing and feedback under Article 4(3).
- The guidance is not legally binding. Only the Court of Justice of the European Union can give a binding interpretation.
