# CRA voluntary reporting of vulnerabilities, threats and incidents

Art. 15 CRA ("Freiwillige Meldungen") opens a voluntary reporting channel next to
the manufacturer's mandatory reporting under Art. 14. Anyone - the manufacturer
or any other natural or legal person ("andere natürliche oder juristische
Personen") - may report to a CSIRT designated as coordinator ("als Koordinator
benanntes CSIRT") or to ENISA:

- **Para. 1** - any vulnerability ("Schwachstelle") contained in a product with
  digital elements, and cyber threats ("Cyberbedrohungen") that could affect the
  product's risk profile.
- **Para. 2** - any security incident ("Sicherheitsvorfall") affecting the
  product's security, and near misses ("Beinahe-Vorfälle") that could have led to
  one.
- **Para. 3** - the CSIRT or ENISA handles these reports under the Art. 16
  procedure; the coordinating CSIRT may prioritise mandatory over voluntary
  reports.
- **Para. 4** - if someone other than the manufacturer reports an actively
  exploited vulnerability ("aktiv ausgenutzte Schwachstelle") or a severe
  incident ("schwerwiegender Sicherheitsvorfall"), the coordinating CSIRT informs
  the manufacturer without undue delay ("unverzüglich").
- **Para. 5** - CSIRTs and ENISA ensure confidentiality and appropriate
  protection of the reporter's information. Without prejudice to the prevention,
  investigation, detection and prosecution of criminal offences, a voluntary
  report must not impose on the reporter any additional obligations it would not
  have had without reporting.

Practical reading from the page (Kunnus and Commission guidance C(2026) 5252
final, both non-binding): voluntary reporting is the route for open-source
stewards giving only non-technical support, and for manufacturers whose
third-party component has an actively exploited vulnerability that is not
exploitable in their own product (plus upstream reporting under Art. 13(6)).
The Kunnus FAQ claims good-faith reporters are shielded from civil and criminal
liability; the article text itself only states the confidentiality and
no-additional-obligations protections of para. 5.
