**Article 15 (Voluntary reporting), Regulation (EU) 2024/2847**

- **Paragraphs 1 and 2:** Manufacturers and other natural or legal persons may voluntarily report vulnerabilities, cyber threats affecting a product's risk profile, security incidents, and near misses. Reports go to a designated coordinating CSIRT or to ENISA.
- **Paragraph 3:** Reports are handled under the procedure in Article 16. The coordinating CSIRT may prioritize mandatory reports over voluntary ones.
- **Paragraph 4:** If a non-manufacturer reports an actively exploited vulnerability or a serious incident, the coordinating CSIRT informs the manufacturer without delay.
- **Paragraph 5:** CSIRTs and ENISA must keep submitted information confidential and protected. Voluntary reports may not impose obligations on the reporter that would not otherwise apply, though this is subject to crime prevention and prosecution.

**Related Commission guidance (27 July 2026, non-binding)**

- **Section 3.3 (open-source stewards):** Reporting duties depend on the kind of support a steward provides. Non-technical support carries no duty to report actively exploited vulnerabilities. Infrastructure stewards and engineering stewards have progressively wider duties under Article 14.
- **Section 9.1 (reporting obligations):** Article 14 reporting applies since 11 September 2026. The clock starts once the manufacturer has sufficient certainty after an initial assessment. Reporting duties continue after the support period ends.
- **Section 9.2 (vulnerability handling):** Covers upstream reporting of component vulnerabilities, checking whether a vulnerability is already known before reporting, and documenting release decisions for late-discovered vulnerabilities.

**Caveat on the page's FAQ**

The FAQ says reporters acting in good faith are protected from civil and criminal liability. The Article 15 text quoted on the page does not state this explicitly.
