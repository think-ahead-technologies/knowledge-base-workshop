# ENISA use of CRA notifications

Art. 17 CRA defines three onward uses by ENISA of the notifications received
under Art. 14(1) and (3) (mandatory reports of actively exploited
vulnerabilities and severe incidents) and Art. 15(1) and (2) (voluntary reports):

- **Forwarding to EU-CyCLONe (para. 1)** - ENISA *may* pass reported information
  to the "Europäische Netzwerk der Verbindungsorganisationen für Cyberkrisen
  (EU-CyCLONe)" (set up by Art. 16 NIS2) if it is relevant "für das koordinierte
  Management massiver Cybersicherheitsvorfälle und -krisen auf operativer Ebene".
  To judge relevance ENISA may take technical analyses of the CSIRTs network into
  account.
- **Trend report every 24 months (para. 3)** - ENISA prepares "alle 24 Monate
  einen technischen Bericht über aufkommende Trends der Cybersicherheitsrisiken
  bei Produkten mit digitalen Elementen" and submits it to the NIS2 Cooperation
  Group (Art. 14 NIS2). The first report is due within 24 months after the
  Art. 14(1) and (3) obligations start to apply. Relevant content also goes into
  ENISA's report on the state of cybersecurity in the Union (Art. 18 NIS2).
- **European vulnerability database (para. 5)** - once a security update or other
  corrective or mitigating measure is available, ENISA, in agreement with the
  manufacturer ("im Einvernehmen mit dem Hersteller"), enters the publicly known
  vulnerability reported under Art. 14(1) or Art. 15(1) into the European
  vulnerability database (Art. 12(2) NIS2).

Takeaway for manufacturers: a reported vulnerability will end up in the EU
vulnerability database after a fix exists, with the manufacturer's agreement -
plan disclosure and advisories accordingly.
