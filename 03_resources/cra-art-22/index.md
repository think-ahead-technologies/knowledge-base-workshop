# CRA Art. 22 - Other cases in which obligations of manufacturers apply (Sonstige Fälle, in denen die Pflichten der Hersteller gelten)

## Summary
Article 22 of the EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, Chapter II
"Pflichten der Wirtschaftsakteure und Bestimmungen in Bezug auf freie und
quelloffene Software") covers third parties that are not the manufacturer,
importer or distributor: a natural or legal person who carries out a
"wesentliche Änderung" (substantial modification) of a product with digital
elements and makes it available on the market "gilt für die Zwecke dieser
Verordnung als Hersteller" (para. 1). That person is bound by the manufacturer
obligations of Art. 13 and 14 for the part affected by the modification, or for
the whole product if the modification affects the product's cybersecurity as a
whole (para. 2). The page is the German text as republished by Kunnus, plus a
summary of the Commission guidance of 27 July 2026 (non-binding) and one related
recital; only the Official Journal text is binding.

## Topics
- [Third party substantially modifying a product becomes manufacturer](resource/cra-art-22/cra-substantial-modification-by-third-party){"type":"related","link-info":"who is deemed manufacturer under Art. 22 and the scope (affected part vs. whole product) of their Art. 13/14 obligations"}
- **Commission guidance, section 4.4 (C(2026) 5252 final, non-binding interpretation summarised by Kunnus)** - a substantially modified product counts as newly placed on the market; integration of components into an own new product is regular manufacturing, not a substantial modification of someone else's product (example 51); substantial modification after 11 December 2027 of a legacy product makes the modifier a manufacturer (Art. 69(2)); original manufacturer's obligations continue; conformity assessment focuses on modified parts, existing tests and documentation may be reused for unchanged parts.
- **Vendor practice note (Kunnus "Für die Praxis", non-binding)** - separate the two cases: substantially modifying a third-party product already on the market (obligations for the modified part only, as long as overall cybersecurity is unaffected) vs. assembling components into an own product (full manufacturer).
- **Related recitals** - page lists recital 85 (truncated excerpt about a reasonable period for publishing harmonised-standard references under Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012; not specific to Art. 22).
