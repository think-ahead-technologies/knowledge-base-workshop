**Article 24 (Obligations of open-source software stewards)**

- It creates a category of "open-source software steward": a legal person that systematically supports FOSS development and commercially exploits it. Stewards carry reduced obligations compared with manufacturers.
- **Paragraph 1:** Stewards must develop and document a verifiable cybersecurity strategy. It should support secure product development and effective vulnerability handling, encourage voluntary vulnerability reporting under Article 15, and promote sharing of vulnerability information within the open-source community.
- **Paragraph 2:** On request, stewards must cooperate with market surveillance authorities to mitigate cybersecurity risks, and provide the required documentation in a form the authority can easily understand.
- **Paragraph 3:** Article 14(1) duties apply to stewards involved in developing products with digitally integrated elements. Article 14(3) and (8) duties apply to serious incidents affecting networks and information systems the steward provides for that development.

**Commission guidance (27 July 2026, Section 3.3, non-binding)**

The guidance scales obligations by the type of support a steward provides:

- **Non-technical support** (branding, governance, events, donations): no mandatory reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities. Information should be passed to maintainers, and voluntary reporting under Article 15 should be considered.
- **Infrastructure steward** (repositories, version control, signing keys): report serious incidents affecting product security to ENISA or the CSIRT, and inform users where appropriate.
- **Engineering steward** (developers, releases, vulnerability handling): report actively exploited vulnerabilities and inform users.
- The role applies per FOSS project, so one organization can be a steward for one project and a manufacturer for another.
- Non-profit entities whose statutory purpose is charitable do not place their FOSS on the market even if they monetize it, so steward duties apply to them.

**FAQ points**

- Examples of stewards given include the Apache Software Foundation, Eclipse Foundation, and Linux Foundation.
- Non-commercial open-source software generally remains outside the CRA. Obligations arise when a commercial actor integrates or distributes it in a product with digital elements, and then fall on that integrating manufacturer.
