**Article 28: EU declaration of conformity (CRA, Chapter III)**

- **Issuance (1):** The manufacturer issues the EU declaration of conformity, confirming that the essential cybersecurity requirements in Annex I have been demonstrated to be met.
- **Format and languages (2):** The full declaration follows the Annex V template and includes the elements from the relevant conformity assessment procedures in Annex VIII. A simplified declaration follows Annex VI. Both must be in the language(s) required by the Member State where the product is placed on the market. The declaration is updated as needed.
- **Multiple laws (3):** If several EU laws require a declaration for the same product, one combined declaration covers all of them and cites the relevant acts and their Official Journal references.
- **Responsibility (4):** Issuing the declaration makes the manufacturer responsible for the product's conformity.
- **Delegated acts (5):** The Commission may adopt delegated acts to add minimum content elements to Annex V, in light of technical developments.

**Commission guidance, section 7.4: product families**

- Variants with the same architecture, security-relevant design, intended purpose, and risk profile can share one risk assessment, technical documentation, conformity assessment, and declaration.
- Differences that don't affect cybersecurity, such as color, form factor, or storage size, are irrelevant.
- Differences that do matter, such as communication interfaces, software stacks, update mechanisms, or remote connectivity, must be reflected in the risk assessment and documentation.
- The shared declaration must clearly identify the covered variants. New variants with new risks require an update.

The page notes that this guidance is not legally binding and that only the Court of Justice of the European Union can give binding interpretations.
