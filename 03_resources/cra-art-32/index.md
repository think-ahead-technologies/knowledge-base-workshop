# CRA Art. 32 - Conformity assessment procedures for products with digital elements

## Summary
Article 32 of the EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847), Chapter III
("Konformität des Produkts mit digitalen Elementen"). It obliges the manufacturer to
carry out a conformity assessment ("Konformitätsbewertung") of the product and of
its own processes against the essential cybersecurity requirements of Annex I, and
fixes which procedures are allowed per product category: any of Module A, Module
B+C, Module H or a European cybersecurity certification scheme for default
products (para. 1); Module B+C or H for Class I important products when harmonised
standards, common specifications or certification schemes (at least assurance
level "mittel") are not or only partly applied or do not exist (para. 2); Module
B+C, H or a certification scheme at least "mittel" for Class II (para. 3); a
certification scheme under Art. 8(1), else a para. 3 procedure, for critical
products of Annex IV (para. 4). FOSS products in Annex III categories may use the
para. 1 procedures if the technical documentation is public (para. 5), and fees
must be reduced proportionally for micro, small and medium-sized enterprises
including start-ups (para. 6). The Kunnus page adds its rendering of Commission
guidance C(2026) 5252 final sections 2.7 and 6.2.

- **Vendor interpretation (Kunnus, non-binding)** - the page has no FAQ; it presents Kunnus' own condensed rendering ("Kurzfassung, Kernaussagen, Für die Praxis") of Commission guidance of 27 July 2026, C(2026) 5252 final. This is vendor paraphrase of a non-binding guidance, not legal text. Points as captured:
  - Section 2.7: products designed before 11 December 2027 but placed on the market afterwards need no forced redesign if a current risk assessment shows existing measures address the risks; conformity assessment, EU declaration of conformity and CE marking remain mandatory; historical design/test documentation need not be reconstructed (Example 12, microcontroller).
  - Section 6.2: Class II and critical products need third-party assessment; Class I may use Module A when a harmonised standard is fully applied that covers at least all risks of the core functionality, with further risks addressed by additional documented measures; FOSS Class I/II products may use default-category procedures (Art. 32(5)); antivirus with extra features may self-assess (Example 62); an integrated critical component does not pull the whole product into its stricter procedure, the core functionality of the overall product decides (Example 63, router with firewall component); breaches of Art. 32 can trigger fines under Art. 64(3).
