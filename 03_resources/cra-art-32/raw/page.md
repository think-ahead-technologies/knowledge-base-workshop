**Article 32: Conformity assessment procedures for products with digital elements (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847)**

1. **General rule:** Manufacturers assess whether a product meets the essential cybersecurity requirements in Annex I, using one of four routes:
   - (a) Internal control (Module A)
   - (b) EU-type examination (Module B), followed by internal production control (Module C)
   - (c) Full quality assurance (Module H)
   - (d) A European cybersecurity certification scheme under Article 27(9), where one exists and applies
2. **Class I important products (Annex III):** Harmonised standards, common specifications, or certification schemes at "substantial" assurance can be applied. If they are missing or only partly applied, the product must follow Module B+C or Module H.
3. **Class II important products:** Module B+C, Module H, or a certification scheme at "substantial" assurance level.
4. **Critical products (Annex IV):** A certification scheme under Article 8(1), or, if those conditions aren't met, one of the paragraph 3 procedures.
5. **Free and open-source software:** Products in the Annex III categories can use the paragraph 1 procedures, provided the technical documentation under Article 31 is publicly available when the product is placed on the market.
6. **Fees:** Conformity assessment fees must account for the particular interests of micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises, including startups, and be reduced proportionally.

**European Commission guidance (27 July 2026, C(2026) 5252 final, non-binding)**

- **Section 2.7, products developed before CRA applies:** No mandatory redesign. The manufacturer runs a current risk assessment, and if existing measures cover the risks, conformity can rest on them. Conformity assessment, EU declaration of conformity, and CE marking remain required. Vulnerability handling, the current risk assessment, and user information still apply in full. The guidance gives a microcontroller example (Example 12).
- **Section 6.2, conformity assessment for important and critical products:**
  - Class II and critical products need third-party assessment.
  - Class I products may use Module A if a harmonised standard fully applied covers the core functionality. Risks beyond that need documented additional measures.
  - FOSS important products of Classes I and II may use the default procedures.
  - An antivirus product with extra features can self-assess if the standard covers its core functionality and the additional risks are documented (Example 62).
  - An integrated critical component does not move the whole product into the stricter procedure. The core functionality of the overall product is decisive (Example 63, router with firewall).
  - Violations of Article 32 can lead to fines under Article 64(3).
