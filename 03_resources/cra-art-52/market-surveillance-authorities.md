# CRA market surveillance authorities

Art. 52 makes the general EU market surveillance regulation, Regulation (EU)
2019/1020, applicable to all products with digital elements in CRA scope
(para 1). Each Member State designates one or more "Marktüberwachungsbehörden";
it may reuse an existing authority or create a new one (para 2).

What the authorities get and must do:

- **Resources (para 8)** - Member States must equip them with adequate financial
  and technical resources, "gegebenenfalls auch mit Tools zur
  Prozessautomatisierung", and staff with the necessary cybersecurity skills.
- **Experience exchange (para 9)** - the Commission promotes and facilitates
  exchange between the designated authorities.
- **Guidance for operators (para 10)** - authorities may give
  "Wirtschaftsakteuren" guidelines and advice on applying the CRA, supported by
  the Commission and, where relevant, CSIRTs and ENISA.
- **Consumer information (para 11)** - in line with Art. 11 of Regulation (EU)
  2019/1020, they tell consumers where to file complaints about possible
  non-compliance, and where and how to reach mechanisms for reporting
  vulnerabilities, security incidents and cyber threats.
- **Stakeholders (para 12)** - facilitate cooperation with relevant stakeholders,
  including science, research and consumer organisations.
- **Reporting (para 13)** - report annually to the Commission on the results of
  their surveillance activities, and pass on without delay to the Commission and
  national competition authorities any information relevant to Union competition
  law.

Takeaway for manufacturers: the CRA is enforced nationally by the same kind of
authority that runs product market surveillance today, with explicit
cybersecurity staffing and an annual reporting loop to the Commission.
