**Article 52: Market surveillance and control of products with digital elements on the Union market**
**Chapter V: Market surveillance and enforcement**
**Source:** Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act)

**Paragraphs**

1. Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 on market surveillance applies to products with digital elements covered by the CRA.
2. Each Member State designates one or more market surveillance authorities, which may be existing or newly created bodies.
3. These authorities also supervise open-source software stewards under Article 24. If a steward fails its obligations, the authority requires corrective measures, and stewards must ensure they are taken.
4. Authorities cooperate with national cybersecurity certification authorities (Cybersecurity Act, Art. 58). For reporting obligations under Article 14, they work with CSIRT coordinators and ENISA.
5. Authorities can request technical advice from CSIRT coordinators or ENISA, including analysis supporting conformity assessments during investigations under Article 54.
6. Authorities cooperate with those designated under other Union harmonisation legislation.
7. Authorities cooperate with data protection authorities, sharing relevant findings. Those data protection authorities may access CRA documentation needed for their tasks and must inform the relevant market surveillance authority of such requests.
8. Member States must provide authorities with adequate financial, technical, and staffing resources, including cybersecurity expertise and, where appropriate, process automation tools.
9. The Commission promotes and facilitates the exchange of experience among market surveillance authorities.
10. Authorities may issue guidance and advice to economic operators, with support from the Commission and, where relevant, CSIRTs and ENISA.
11. Authorities inform consumers where to file complaints about possible non-compliance and where to find vulnerability and incident reporting mechanisms.
12. Authorities facilitate cooperation with stakeholders, including science, research, and consumer organisations.
13. Authorities report annually to the Commission on their surveillance activities. They must also promptly pass on information relevant to Union competition law to the Commission and national competition authorities.
14. For products classified as high-risk AI systems under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act, Art. 6), the AI Act market surveillance authorities are also responsible for CRA surveillance. They cooperate with CRA authorities, CSIRTs, and ENISA, and share relevant findings.
15. The ADCO (administrative cooperation group), established under Article 30(2) of Regulation 2019/1020, ensures uniform application. It includes authority representatives and also handles questions on open-source steward obligations.
16. Authorities monitor how manufacturers apply the criteria in Article 13(8) when setting support periods. The ADCO publishes accessible statistics and indicative guidance on support periods by product category and may recommend that authorities focus on categories with insufficient support.
