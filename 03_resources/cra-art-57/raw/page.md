**Article 57: Compliant products with digital elements that present a significant cybersecurity risk**

- **Chapter:** V, Market surveillance and enforcement
- **Source:** Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act), published 10 December 2024
- **Last reviewed by Kunnus:** March 2026
- **Navigation:** Previous article is Article 56 (procedure at Union level for products with digital elements). Next article is Article 58 (formal non-compliance).

**Paragraph summary**

1. A member state's market surveillance authority can require an economic operator to take all appropriate measures. This applies when, after an evaluation under Article 54, the authority finds that a product complies with the regulation and the manufacturer's procedures, yet still poses a significant cybersecurity risk. The risks listed are: harm to health or safety, risk to fundamental-rights obligations, risk to the availability or integrity of services from essential entities under Directive (EU) 2022/2555, and risk to other public-interest concerns. Measures may ensure the risk no longer exists when the product is placed on, withdrawn from, or recalled from the market.
2. Manufacturers and other relevant operators must take corrective measures for all affected products they have placed on the EU market, within the deadline set by the authority.
3. The member state must promptly inform the Commission and other member states of measures taken, including product identification, origin and supply chain, type of risk, and the nature and duration of national measures.
4. The Commission must promptly consult member states and the operator, review the national measures, and decide whether they are justified. It may propose appropriate measures.
5. The Commission addresses its decision from paragraph 4 to the member states.
6. If the Commission has sufficient reason to believe a compliant product still poses these risks, it can notify the relevant authorities and ask them to carry out an evaluation and apply the procedures. This can draw on information from ENISA.
7. In urgent cases needed to protect the smooth functioning of the internal market, where authorities have not acted effectively, the Commission can carry out its own risk assessment. It can ask ENISA for analysis and must inform the authorities. Operators cooperate with ENISA as needed.
8. Based on that assessment, the Commission can decide that a Union-level corrective or restrictive measure is needed, after consulting the affected member states and operators.
9. Following that consultation, the Commission can adopt implementing acts imposing Union-level measures, including withdrawal from the market or recall within a period suited to the risk. These follow the examination procedure in Article 62(2).
10. The Commission promptly informs the affected operators of these implementing acts. Member states implement them without delay and report back to the Commission.
11. Paragraphs 6 to 10 apply only while the exceptional circumstances justifying Commission intervention last, and while the product still poses the risks in paragraph 1.
