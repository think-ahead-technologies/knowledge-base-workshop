**Artikel 63: Vertraulichkeit**
*Kapitel VII: Vertraulichkeit und Sanktionen, Verordnung (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act)*

**Absatz 1:** All parties involved in applying the regulation must keep confidential the information they learn in performing their tasks. In particular, they must protect:
- (a) intellectual property, confidential business information, and trade secrets, including source code, except as provided in Article 5 of Directive (EU) 2016/943;
- (b) the effective enforcement of the regulation, especially for inspections, investigations, or audits;
- (c) public and national security interests;
- (d) the integrity of criminal or administrative proceedings.

**Absatz 2:** Market surveillance authorities that share information confidentially with each other or with the Commission may not pass it on without the consent of the authority that provided it.

**Absatz 3:** Paragraphs 1 and 2 do not affect the rights and duties of the Commission, Member States, and notified bodies regarding information exchange and warnings. They also do not affect duties to provide information under Member States' criminal law.

**Absatz 4:** The Commission and Member States may exchange sensitive information with authorities in third countries where bilateral or multilateral confidentiality agreements ensure an adequate level of protection, if necessary.

**Related recitals (partial on the page):**
- **Recital 121:** When fines are imposed on a non-company person, the authority should consider the general income level in the relevant Member State.
- **Recital 122:** Member States should consider whether revenue from sanctions under the regulation can be used for purposes that serve its goals.
