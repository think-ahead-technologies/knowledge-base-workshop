**Artikel 64: Sanktionen (Kapitel VII, Vertraulichkeit und Sanktionen)**
Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, published 10 December 2024. The page says it was last reviewed by Kunnus in March 2026.

**Quick answer:** Article 64 sets penalties for CRA violations. The maximum is EUR 15 million or 2.5% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Member states implement the penalties nationally.

**Legal text (paragraphs 1-10), summarized:**
1. Member states must set penalties that are effective, proportionate, and dissuasive, and notify the Commission.
2. Violating the essential cybersecurity requirements (Annex I) or the obligations in Articles 13 and 14: up to EUR 15M or 2.5% of turnover.
3. Violating the obligations in Articles 18-23, 28, 30(1-4), 31(1-4), 32(1-3), 33(5), 39, 41, 47, 49, and 53: up to EUR 10M or 2% of turnover.
4. Providing false, incomplete, or misleading information to notified bodies or market surveillance authorities on request: up to EUR 5M or 1% of turnover.
5. Fines must consider the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, prior fines for similar violations, and the size and market share of the operator, especially micro, small, and medium enterprises.
6. Authorities imposing fines must inform other member states' authorities via the information system under Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, Article 34.
7. Member states decide whether and to what extent public authorities can be fined.
8. Fines may be imposed by courts or other bodies, depending on each member state's legal system, with equivalent effect.
9. Fines can be imposed alongside corrective or restrictive measures for the same violation.
10. Paragraphs 3-9 do not apply to (a) micro and small manufacturers regarding certain reporting deadlines under Article 14(2)(a) or 14(4)(a), and (b) stewards of open-source software for any violation.

**Commission guidance (27 July 2026, C(2026) 5252 final), Section 6.2: Conformity assessment of important and critical products**
- Class II and critical products require third-party assessment. Class I important products may use internal control (Module A) if a harmonized standard fully covers the core functionality. Additional risks need documented measures.
- Open-source important products in classes I and II may use the default procedures (Art. 32(5)).
- Antivirus products with extra features may self-assess if the standard covers the core risks and the additional risks are documented.
- A critical component integrated into a product does not automatically move the whole product into its stricter procedure. The product's core functionality is decisive (router with firewall example).
- Violations of Article 32 can trigger fines under Article 64(3).

The guidance is not legally binding. Only the Court of Justice of the EU can give binding interpretations.

**FAQ summary:**
- **Maximum fines:** Up to EUR 15M or 2.5% for core obligations; up to EUR 10M or 2% for less severe violations; up to EUR 5M or 1% for obstructing market surveillance.
- **Factors affecting the amount:** severity and duration, number of affected users, intent or negligence, cooperation, and mitigation measures. The page says SME size is a mitigating factor (recital 102).
- **Existential threat:** The page says large fines are rarely reached by large companies, and that accompanying measures such as sales bans, recalls, and reputational damage may be more threatening.

**Related recitals:** recital 123 (international trade with regulated products) and recital 124 (consumer rights through collective actions under Directive (EU) 2020/1828).
